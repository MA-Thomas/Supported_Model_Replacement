       Supported Model Replacement Across Target Prevalences
       An Observed-Anchored Hierarchy for Empirical and Computational Challenge


                                          Marcus A. Thomas

                                       Methodological proposal


                                                 Abstract

         A decision to replace an incumbent prediction model often begins with an observed per-
     formance diﬀerence. A stronger claim asks whether that advantage survives the conditions,
     empirical evaluations, and computational perturbations under which the claimant has agreed
     that it may fail. These challenges have diﬀerent scientiﬁc identities. A target condition is evalu-
     ated within a full evaluation, a computational replication recombines the evidence represented
     in one empirical evaluation, and a separately conducted empirical evaluation supplies another
     test of the claim.
         We develop an observed-anchored hierarchy that preserves those identities. A paired eﬀect
     ﬁrst faces every declared target condition. Several empirical eﬀects then face an across-evaluation
     challenge, which acts as a necessary empirical gate. Only a claim that passes that gate proceeds
     to a declared within-evaluation computational challenge. Every nested challenge retains the
     eﬀect actually observed in each selected empirical evaluation as a mandatory anchor. Computa-
     tional replication can therefore preserve or reduce the support credited to the observed evidence,
     but cannot erase an observed failure or multiply the number of empirical tests.
         The construction yields supported magnitude and literal survival of a declared eﬀect ﬂoor.
     An exact survival representation shows that both summarize the same complete nested chal-
     lenges. We develop the method for a paired, tie-averaged, prior-standardized form of average
     precision across target prevalences. A ﬁnite-evidence replacement rule requires both the ob-
     served empirical gate and, when declared, the full anchored assessment to clear prespeciﬁed
     thresholds. The rule does not estimate a probability of success in unseen evaluations. It records
     what survived the attempted refutations actually represented by the assessment.
         Bank Marketing illustrates the anchored one-evaluation boundary. ProteinGym supplies
     91 observed assay evaluations whose comparisons fail at the empirical gate, making further
     computational challenge unnecessary for their verdicts.



1    Performance claims and levels of challenge

Prediction systems often rank cases so that a selected set can receive further attention. A radiology
service may send its highest-scored images for review; a screening program may investigate its
highest-scored records ﬁrst. The composition of that selected set matters because it is what the
decision maker receives.
Separation and selected-set purity part company when prevalence changes. Suppose a threshold
selects 80% of positive cases and 5% of negative cases. In a balanced population of 10,000 cases
it returns 4,000 positives and 250 negatives, for precision 0.94. In a population with 1% positives,
the same threshold returns 80 positives and 495 negatives, for precision 0.14. Its true-positive and


                                                     1
false-positive rates have not moved. The supply of negative cases has, and that is enough to change
the meaning of the selected set.
Average precision aggregates precision as a ranking is traversed. Ordinary empirical AP therefore
evaluates the ranking at the positive fraction in the evaluation data. A performance claim intended
for several deployment populations must therefore name the target prevalences within its scope. No
single convenient prevalence establishes a claim throughout a range, and an average over prevalences
permits a favorable population to compensate for an unfavorable one.
Call Dm a full empirical evaluation when it contains the outcomes and scores needed to evaluate
every declared target condition on one set of units. A second opportunity for failure remains inside
each full empirical evaluation. A conclusion may depend on the realized combination of represented
units. A declared computational procedure can expose that dependence by recombining those units.
Call each resulting derived collection a computational evaluation. It is distinct from the empirical
evaluation from which it is derived, and it is full when it contains the outcomes and scores needed
to evaluate every declared target condition. It supplies an additional challenge conditional on
the empirical evidence; it does not supply another empirical evaluation or introduce a case type,
environment, score value, measurement process, or failure mechanism that the empirical evaluation
did not contain. The unqualiﬁed phrase full evaluation refers to either a full empirical evaluation
or a full computational evaluation.
A third opportunity for failure arises when the performance claim is evaluated again. A level
retained in one full empirical evaluation may fail in another admissible collection of units or context.
A claim intended to survive such tests must name an evaluation regime and then face the empirical
evaluations conducted within it.
These three routes of attempted refutation have diﬀerent scientiﬁc identities:

   1. a condition challenge asks what eﬀect survives every declared condition within a full evalua-
      tion;

   2. a computational challenge asks what remains under declared recombinations within one em-
      pirical evaluation; and

   3. an empirical challenge asks what remains across separately conducted evaluations admitted
      by the evaluation regime.

They share one noncompensation principle: form a complete challenge before averaging what sur-
vived it.
At ﬁrst, this nesting seems to prescribe an inside-out calculation: challenge the conditions, then the
computational recombinations, and ﬁnally the empirical evaluations. That order would, however,
allow derived computational results to enter before the observed empirical evidence has established
that there is anything to preserve. Computational recombination can deepen the challenge applied
to an empirical evaluation, but it cannot repair an observed empirical failure. The construction
therefore reverses the last two steps. It ﬁrst asks what observed eﬀect survives across the em-
pirical evaluations. Only a claim that survives that empirical challenge is then exposed to the
additional within-evaluation computational challenge. This diﬀerence between structural nesting
and evidential order motivates the observed anchor developed below.

Increasing Jm reﬁnes a computational approximation. Increasing KC changes the challenge itself.

is challenged. No choice of Jm turns one empirical evaluation into several.
The motivating application is model replacement. Candidate A and incumbent B are evaluated on
the same units, and larger paired eﬀects favor the named orientation. Pairing removes the possibility
that the two models meet their adverse conditions in diﬀerent populations or evaluations. It also
exposes a further problem: ﬁnite-sample AP can reward whichever model divides tied scores more
ﬁnely, even when that reﬁnement is unrelated to the outcomes. The next section constructs one
retained paired AP eﬀect before any empirical or computational eﬀects are combined.


2        Constructing a retained paired AP eﬀect

The hierarchy will act on eﬀects that have already faced every declared target condition. We ﬁrst
construct one such eﬀect from one full evaluation.


2.1      Prior-standardized average precision

Let D contain outcomes and the two ﬁxed score vectors on a common set of N evaluation units.

2.4   Tie-averaged average precision

an outcome-blind vector of distinct scores creates more opportunities for favorable positive entries.
Pairing alone does not remove that advantage.

4     A staged ﬁnite-evidence replacement rule

The operator describes what survived the declared challenges. A replacement policy must also state
how much retained advantage and how much literal survival are suﬃcient.
Let δ ≥ 0 be the smallest supported magnitude that would justify replacement. Let d ≥ 0 be the
ﬂoor used to deﬁne literal survival, and let γ ∈ (0, 1) be the minimum acceptable survival fraction.
These are policy commitments, not conﬁdence levels or quantities learned from the eﬀect array.

5     Evidence and computational implementation

The hierarchy preserves a diﬀerence that no algebra should erase. Empirical evaluations supply
evidence. Computational replications act on that evidence under a declared procedure. The same
numerical eﬀect may appear at either level, but its index records a diﬀerent attempted refutation.


5.1   The empirical evaluation regime

The regime R states when a full empirical evaluation counts as an admissible test of the claim.
It includes the object under test, eligible populations, measurement standards, available outcomes
and scores, and the independent evaluation unit.
A separately conducted full empirical evaluation satisfying R is an empirical replication. Its units
do not overlap those in another replication, and its model construction and data collection are
not altered in response to another evaluation in the same set. Resampling draws, administrative
partitions, cross-validation folds, and repeated analyses of the same units do not create empirical
replications.

                                                  14
A ﬁxed-model regime holds the ﬁtted models and measurement process ﬁxed while admitting new
evaluation units. A broader regime may admit distinct environments or a prespeciﬁed model-
updating procedure. When an earlier evaluation prompts a later update, the evaluations form an
adaptive sequence; they are not a set of separate replications under the deﬁnition above.
Several evaluations may occur in diﬀerent contexts while addressing the same scoped claim. Their
identities should be retained when contextual variation is scientiﬁcally meaningful. If dataset
membership is merely administrative and all units address one aggregate population, pooling may be
more appropriate. The regime supplies admissibility; it does not assert that admissible evaluations
are draws from a common population law.


5.2   The within-evaluation computational procedure

The procedure B declares how computational evaluations are generated within each empirical row.
For the AP illustration, it samples the declared numbers of units with replacement within outcome
class, keeps both model scores attached to every sampled unit, and evaluates the complete paired
prevalence proﬁle. Stratiﬁcation ﬁxes the class counts; a prospective projection may instead use
counts planned for a later evaluation. Clustered or matched studies must resample declared design
blocks rather than their component observations.
The resampling law is a constructed computational measure on recombinations of represented units.
It can expose dependence on the realized mix of those units. It cannot produce an unseen case
type or a failure caused by a new environment, measurement process, development run, or scoring
mechanism.
If empirical evaluations have nonoverlapping units and are resampled separately, independent com-
putational selections across rows give the product in Equation (19). If evaluations share units or a
scientiﬁcally meaningful perturbation must be coordinated across contexts, the joint computational
law must preserve that dependence. The direct challenge deﬁnition remains valid, but the product
representation may not.

A ﬁnite condition set deﬁnes an exact ﬁnite challenge. A grid used merely to approximate a
continuous prevalence interval can miss an interior minimum and raise an eﬀect. A one-dimensional

6.2   Several empirical evaluations: ProteinGym

Deep-mutational-scanning (DMS) assays experimentally measure the functional eﬀects of many
amino-acid substitutions in a protein. ProteinGym collects and standardizes these measurements
together with released prediction scores [13]. The task here is to rank single substitutions so that
experimentally deleterious or nonfunctional variants appear ahead of functional variants. Such rank-
ings can prioritize substitutions for further interpretation or experimental study when exhaustive
measurement is unavailable.
We compared released zero-shot scores from three methodologically distinct protein sequence mod-
els: the EVE ensemble evolutionary model, the ESM-1v ensemble protein language model, and the
ESM-2 650M protein language model [8, 11, 10]. The comparisons were ESM-1v ensemble against
EVE ensemble, ESM-2 650M against ESM-1v ensemble, and ESM-2 650M against EVE ensemble.
No model was ﬁt or tuned for this study. The replacement question was whether one released rank-
ing retained a positive advantage throughout the target deleterious-prevalence interval and across
a suﬃciently large fraction of complete assay evaluations.
Each DMS assay supplied one full empirical evaluation because it contained a complete paired
comparison of the model scores and experimental labels in a particular assay setting. Individual
substitutions were evaluation units within an assay; they were not empirical replications. The
across-evaluation challenge therefore asked whether an advantage persisted across assays rather
than across variants pooled from those assays. Pooling all substitutions would allow larger assays to
dominate and would conceal whether an average advantage persisted across distinct assay settings.
We used ProteinGym version 1.3 substitution data, retained assays with manual biological cutoﬀs,
and retained single amino-acid substitutions. Experimentally nonfunctional or deleterious variants
formed the positive class. Because the released scores were oriented toward predicted ﬁtness, they
were negated so that larger analyzed values represented stronger predicted deleteriousness. The
aligned cohort contained 277,873 substitutions in 91 assays, including 96,987 deleterious and 180,886
functional variants.
The observed deleterious fraction ranged from 0.025 to 0.869 across assays, with median 0.315
and interquartile range [0.244, 0.440]. All 91 assay fractions lay within the declared target interval
[0.01, 0.90]. The pooled fraction was 96,987/277,873 = 0.349, but this value weights assays by their

7                  Interpretation, reporting, and scope

7.1                  What the quantities support

Observed support describes what the separately conducted empirical evaluations retain after the
condition challenge. Anchored empirical–computational support describes what remains when each
selected empirical evaluation must retain both its observed anchor and selected computational
eﬀects. Neither is a portable property of a model. Both are indexed by the orientation, condition set,
evaluation regime, challenge orders, and—for the full construction—the computational procedure.
The hierarchy is falsiﬁcation-oriented. It asks how much of a claim remains after named oppor-
tunities for failure have been imposed. Passing means that the claim survived those attempted
refutations. It does not establish truth beyond them [14, 15]. In particular, the ﬁnite empirical list
is not treated as a random sample from a latent population of evaluations, and the nested survival
fraction is not a conﬁdence level or an estimate of future replication probability.
Computational replication deepens but does not broaden the empirical basis. Increasing M adds
empirical tests. Increasing Jm improves numerical approximation. Increasing KE or KC strengthens
a declared challenge. These actions answer diﬀerent questions and should not share the generic
label “more replications.”
The result remains a ranking-performance claim. Calibration, utility, resource constraints, and
consequences of acting on selected cases may determine a deployment decision but are not evaluated
by the replacement rule.

                                                                           22
7.2     Relation to population robustness and inference

Some operations have analogues in frameworks that begin with a population probability law. Shared
algebra does not supply a shared interpretation.
The retained eﬀect inf c∈C ∆c (D) resembles an intersection bound. Intersection-bound inference
instead concerns inﬁma or suprema of population-indexed functions and their repeated-sampling
behavior [5]. Here the inﬁmum extracts the exact uniform lower level of a condition proﬁle computed
in one complete ﬁnite evaluation.
The ordered ﬁnite-list form of −SK resembles a spectral loss functional [1], and the survival integral
resembles a distortion or Choquet representation [19]. In those settings, order statistics commonly
estimate a functional of a random quantity. Here the representations are exact identities for a
declared ﬁnite indexed list or array. No superpopulation interpretation is imported.
The empirical case-mix sets developed in Appendix F resemble ambiguity sets in distributionally
robust optimization [17, 4]. Their concentration factor is a deterministic allowance over represented
cases, not a conﬁdence radius around an unknown distribution. Population coverage results from
data-driven robust optimization require a diﬀerent inferential target and assumptions [7].
Probability is used here when a target population description or deliberately constructed random-
ization requires it. The target prevalence describes a counterfactual population composition. The
resampling law deﬁnes a computational projection. The exchangeable-label law deﬁnes a reference
diagnostic. None is silently promoted into a law generating unseen empirical evaluations.

7.4   Limits and transport commitments

Prior standardization varies the class prior while retaining observed joint class-conditional score
behavior. It does not address changes within outcome class. Computational resampling recombines
represented units. It does not extrapolate to absent units. Additional empirical reach requires
additional evaluations within R, not stronger probabilistic language about the evaluations already
observed.
Tie averaging removes expected credit for outcome-blind reﬁnement within score blocks. It does
not create full label exchangeability. Matched, clustered, and heterogeneous-risk designs may retain
design-speciﬁc reference anchors.
Anchoring deliberately keeps the eﬀect actually obtained in a ﬁnite empirical evaluation binding.
An unusually adverse anchor remains binding even when most resamples are favorable. This is not
treated as bias to be corrected: the computational distribution may expose or describe fragility,
but it does not erase the empirical test.
The choices of C, R, B, KE , and KC remain scientiﬁc commitments. Widening the condition set or
increasing an order mechanically strengthens a ﬁxed challenge, but no such operation proves that
the assessment contains the failures most relevant to deployment.

8     Conclusion

A replacement claim becomes stronger only when the attempted refutations it has survived are
made explicit. For AP, the ﬁrst challenge is a declared set of target prevalences. For empirical
persistence, it is a set of separately conducted evaluations admitted by a regime. For dependence on
the represented units, it may include a scientiﬁcally defensible computational resampling procedure.
These levels can be combined without treating them as exchangeable.
The construction ﬁrst pairs the models, resolves outcome-blind tie reﬁnement, and retains the
weakest advantage over target prevalence in every complete observed or computational evaluation.
It then preserves empirical identity: each selected row keeps its observed retained eﬀect as a
mandatory anchor and adds selected computational eﬀects as further opportunities for failure. The
complete nested challenge is formed before its weakest eﬀect is averaged with the minima of other
challenges.
Observed anchoring gives the direct empirical analysis a precise role. Direct observed support and
literal survival form a necessary gate. Failure there proves that the full anchored rule cannot pass.
A claim that clears the gate proceeds to the computational challenge, where computation may
preserve or reduce the credited support but cannot overwrite the evidence actually obtained.
Supported magnitude and literal survival remain two summaries of the same objects:

F.3   Computation and reporting

Equation (44) minimizes the bilinear form w+⊤ Gw over two capped simplices. A feasible pair of

weights supplies an upper bound on the inﬁmum and can demonstrate policy failure. Establishing

without such a bound may stop above the true inﬁmum and overstate survival.

from that evaluation has weight zero at every Γ. Individual reweighting may also concentrate on
sample-speciﬁc errors rather than a named subpopulation. The breakdown factor consequently
measures survival under a reproducible empirical concentration rule; it is not the magnitude of a
